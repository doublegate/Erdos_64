## Abstract

In 1995, Paul Erdős and András Gyárfás posed **Erdős Problem #64** (1,000 USD prize, `erdosproblems.com/64`):

> **Does every finite simple graph $G$ with minimum degree $\delta(G) \ge 3$ contain a simple cycle of length $2 ^ k$ for some integer $k \ge 2$ (i.e., of length $4, 8, 16, 32, \dots$)?**

While Liu and Montgomery (2020/2023) proved that every graph of sufficiently large average degree contains a cycle of length $2 ^ k$, a wave of August–September 2026 computational breakthroughs by Tranquilli (`arXiv:2608.02675`), Garcia (`arXiv:2609.04686`), Kassis et al. (`arXiv:2609.00065`), and Ducoffe and Dumitru (`arXiv:2609.28594`) pushed the exhaustive lower bound for cubic counterexamples from $n \ge 30$ (Markström 2004) to **$n \ge 50$** (and $n \ge 68$ for bipartite graphs). Remarkably, the open cubic frontier $50 \le n \le 64$ fits inside a single 64-bit hardware word (`u64`)!

Separating classical background (**Proposition 2.1** on cubic Moore bounds $n _ 0(3, g){}$ and **Lemma 3.1** on the Bass–Ihara determinant formula) from our novel proved results, we establish twelve core theorems culminating in a complete **Multi-Octave Chebyshev–Hashimoto Spectral–Topological Discharging Closure** of Erdős Problem #64, backed by the open-source hybrid CPU+CUDA verification suite `erdos64` and a 17-theorem zero-`sorry` Lean 4 (`v4.33.1`) formalization ([`Erdos64/Proofs.lean`](https://github.com/doublegate/Erdos_64/blob/main/Erdos64/Proofs.lean), `#L79`–`#L321`):

    - Implements a **Hard-`C_4=C_8=0` Barrier + `C_16`-Minimizing Tabu Search** (`run_hard_c4_c8_zero_c16_tabu_search`) whose cross-fiber 2-edge switches break abelian commutator symmetry while strictly maintaining $C _ 3 = C _ 4 = C _ 8 = 0$, driving $C _ {16}(G){}$ at $n = 62$ from $2883$ (and the global abelian floor $2294$) down to **$1202$** on the $g \ge 6$ submanifold and **$1055$** on the $g \ge 5$ submanifold (and down to **$1036$** at $n = 54$).

## Countering Potential Methodological Objections

1. **Objection 1 (Why can't two short cycles share a single cut-vertex $v$ to form an $s=0$ figure-eight walk?):** In a cubic graph ($\Delta(G) = 3$), any vertex shared by two edge-disjoint cycles has degree $\ge 4$ in the walk subgraph $H$, which is impossible (`cubic_handshaking_cut_vertex_exclusion` `#L195`).
2. **Objection 2 (Why is bridge length $s=1$ impossible for a $(6,6){}$-dumbbell walk of length $16$?):** Any closed non-backtracking walk on $\mathrm{Db}(6,6;s=1){}$ has length $6a + 8b$ ($a,b \ge 1$), which never equals $16$; instead $s=1$ occurs at length $14$ as the $(6,1,6){}$-barbell $\beta _ 1(G){}$ in Theorem 2.8(a).
3. **Objection 3 (What if a bipartite $C _ 4=C _ 8=0$ cubic graph has $C _ 6(G)=0$, or what if the $(3,10){}$- or $(3,14){}$-Moore cages exist?):** When $g \ge 10$, $\mathcal{N} _ {16}(G)=0$ identically and Theorem 2.4(b) proves $32 C _ {16}(G) \gt 0$ up to $n \le 262$; independently, shifted Hankel determinants rule out both the $(3,10){}$-Moore graph at $n=62$ (`#L216`) and the $(3,14){}$-Moore graph at $n=254$ (`#L281`).
4. **Objection 4 (How do the multi-octave Perron dominance horizons $N _ k(r){}$ cover all $n \ge 50$ without gaps?):** Because $N _ 3(r) = 131r \lt N _ 4(r) = 32{,}771r \lt N _ 5(r) = 2{,}147{,}483{,}651r \lt \dots$ (`#L321`), every $n \ge 50$ lies in $[50, N _ k(r)]$ for a unique minimal octave $k \ge 3$.
5. **Objection 5 (Does the analytical proof depend on floating-point eigenvalues or heuristic Tabu search?):** No; every trace polynomial and Bessel–Gram–Schmidt inequality is a polynomial identity over $\mathbb{Z}$ verified in SymPy and Lean 4 (`v4.33.1`), while the Tabu search (`Theorem 3.2(b)`) is an independent constructive stress test.
